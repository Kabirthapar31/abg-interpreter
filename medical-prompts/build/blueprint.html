02 · Your house style

The Parabox prompt DNA: 8 traits I'll keep in all 1,000.

Across every pack, your prompts share a signature. Your own line puts it best: "written the way a research supervisor thinks, not the way a chatbot answers."

01
A senior persona with credentials and a personality."A biostatistician with two decades of experience… openly critical of common misuse." "A strict but fair examiner." It's never just "You are an expert."
02
A MY CONTEXT block of labelled fill-in fields.Specialty, programme and year, institution type, patient volume, resources you do and don't have. "The AI does not know your patients."
03
A numbered TASK that names exactly what comes out."Generate 8 candidate research questions… for each, give me exactly this: 1… 10."
04
A RULES block against fabrication, with named tags.[CITE], [VERIFY], [ASSUMPTION — VERIFY FROM PUBLISHED DATA], [CITATION NEEDED — FIND SOURCE], [UNVERIFIED BACKGROUND].
05
India-first realism.Government medical college, ICMR 2017, NFHS, CTRI, IEC, NEET-PG / INI-CET, 2-year MD timelines, limited lab infrastructure.
06
A self-critique ending."The three sentences a hostile reviewer would attack first", "AVOID THESE", "act as the ethics committee reviewer", "performance summary".
07
Wrapped in USE IT · WHY · HOW TO GET MORE OUT OF IT.You tell the doctor when to use each prompt, why it exists, and how to push the output further.
08
An honest, direct voice."The AI drafts. You decide." "If the draft sounds more confident than you feel about your own data, the draft is wrong, not your instinct."

04 · The framework

Built on your own P-R-O-M-P-T.

Your Prompt Cheatcode already covers every element the four labs require. Rather than bring in a new acronym, the 1,000 prompts are built on your framework, and every block maps to the lab guidance.

P
Persona
A senior expert with credentials and a trait
Google Persona · OpenAI Identity · Claude role
R
Request
One clear task, as numbered steps
Google Task · Microsoft Goal · OpenAI Instructions
O
Output
Shape, length, tone, tables, slides
Google Format · Microsoft Expectations
M
Material
MY CONTEXT block + [PASTE] slot (first)
Google Context · Microsoft Context + Source
P
Parameters
Rules, guidelines, [CITE] / [VERIFY] guards
OpenAI constraints · Claude "say if unsure"
T
Tweak
Self-critique ending + PRO TIP + → NEXT
All four labs: iterate

In each prompt, the blocks appear in the order the labs recommend (Persona → Material → Request → Parameters → Output → Tweak), with your labels: MY CONTEXT · TASK · RULES · OUTPUT.

05 · Length & mix

Three tiers, matched to the job.

Quick
40–80 words
One focused output: a differential, 20 flashcards, a mnemonic, one slide rewrite. Persona + Request + Output in 2–4 sentences, like your ChatGPT and Ophthalmology prompts.
≈ 300 prompts · 4 per page
Standard · default
100–200 words
One real task done properly: a mini MY CONTEXT, a numbered TASK, one or two RULES, OUTPUT and a PRO TIP. Most of the library.
≈ 580 prompts · 2 per page
Master
250–450 words
Your flagship format for full workflows: protocol, SAP, full ROL, full manuscript, full mock viva. The full USE IT / WHY / TIPS wrapper.
≈ 120 prompts · 1 per page
Context rules (from Step 1, now in your style)
  • 2–5 context fields the AI can't guess: level, specialty, setting, exam or journal, resources.
  • Placeholders in [SQUARE BRACKETS] with a hint, like [e.g. Respiratory Medicine]. Five or fewer in Quick prompts; more are fine in the Master MY CONTEXT block.
  • Anything pasted goes above the TASK. No contradictions. Say what to do, and name the output.

Sample card: Master.

361From interesting case to CARE-compliant case report
Master
PART 1 › 1.12 CASE REPORTS & CASE SERIES · RESIDENT / FACULTY
USE ITAs soon as you suspect a case is unusual, while the notes, images and consent are still easy to get.
WHYMost case reports are rejected because they aren't novel or the timeline is unclear, not because the writing is weak. This prompt checks publishability first.
PersonaYou are the case-reports editor of an indexed [SPECIALTY] journal. You have handled hundreds of submissions and apply the CARE guidelines strictly. You are blunt about whether a case is actually publishable. My contextSpecialty and my level: [e.g. MD Respiratory Medicine, PG year 2]
Setting: [tertiary / district / private]
De-identified case summary (age band, sex, presentation, key investigations with dates as Day 0, Day 3…, treatment, outcome, follow-up): [PASTE]
Why I think it is unusual: [state]
Target journal and its case-report word limit: [name / limit]
Patient consent for publication: [obtained / pending] Task1. Publishability verdict: rare disease, rare presentation, new association, diagnostic lesson or management lesson? Score novelty out of 5 and justify it against my case.
2. Five title options that each contain the words "case report".
3. A CARE-structured draft: abstract (≤150 words), introduction, patient information, clinical findings, timeline table, diagnostic assessment (including the differential you excluded and how), therapeutic intervention, follow-up and outcome, discussion, patient perspective placeholder and learning points.
4. A CARE checklist table showing where each item appears and flagging anything missing.
5. The consent and de-identification checklist I need before submission. RulesUse only the clinical details I gave. Where something is missing, write [ASK ME: …] instead of filling it in. Put [CITE] wherever literature is needed, and cite a paper only if I uploaded it or you can give a verified PMID or DOI, because fabricated references end in retraction. Keep every date relative (Day 0…) and remove anything identifying. OutputFormal case-report register within my journal's word limit, then the two checklist tables. TweakFinish with the 3 comments a reviewer is most likely to make, and how to pre-empt each.
HOW TO GET MORE OUT OF IT
  • If the verdict says "not novel", reframe the case as a teaching image or a letter instead of forcing a full report.
  • Build the timeline table first. Reviewers read it before the text.
  • Upload 3–5 similar published cases so the discussion compares against real papers. → NEXT #366 Case report to conference poster.

08 · The book

How the final PDF is built.

Front matter

  • Cover in the Parabox style
  • Read this first: your 5 non-negotiables
  • How to use each prompt: brackets, fresh chat per stage, push back
  • The P-R-O-M-P-T framework with the Bad → Good → Better demo
  • Quick-find index: "I need to…" → prompt number

Body

  • 3 part openers with section lists
  • 27 section openers, each with a one-line WHY
  • 1,000 numbered prompt cards, with tier badges and → NEXT chains

Back matter

  • Tool-per-task table (Perplexity, Consensus, NotebookLM, Claude, ChatGPT, Julius…)
  • Ethics of AI in research box · closing note · disclaimer

Every prompt passes this QA check

  • Has all six P-R-O-M-P-T blocks (Quick prompts may merge them)
  • The persona has seniority and a trait
  • Word count is inside its tier
  • Paste slot sits above the TASK
  • Output shape and length are named
  • Every "Do not" comes with the action to take instead
  • Has an accuracy guard where facts matter: [CITE], [VERIFY], PMID/DOI rule
  • No patient identifiers, and the de-identification line is present on case prompts
  • No contradictions, and no near-duplicate of another prompt
  • Works unchanged in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Copilot
Size estimate
≈ 300 Quick (4/page) + 580 Standard (2/page) + 120 Master (1/page) ≈ 480–520 pages with openers. See decision 01.

09 · Your call

Four decisions before I start.

Each one has a recommendation. Reply "go with the recommendations" or change any of them.

01
One book or three volumes?At about 500 pages, a single PDF is heavy to share on WhatsApp or through DMs. Three volumes (one per part) plus a combined edition are easier to share and let you release one at a time.
Recommended: three volumes plus a combined edition.
02
Reuse your existing prompts?About 40 of your published prompts (thesis, protocol, ophthalmology exam set, study guide) could become refreshed anchors inside the 1,000, or the library could be 100% new.
Recommended: reuse and refresh them as Master anchors, clearly the upgraded versions (about 4% of the library).
03
Audience scope and exam listYour packs are India-first (ICMR, CTRI, NEET-PG / INI-CET). Should prompts also carry international variants such as USMLE, PLAB, MRCP and FCPS?
Recommended: India-first defaults, with international exams offered as options inside the brackets, e.g. [NEET-PG / INI-CET / USMLE / PLAB / MRCP].
04
Tier mix and split300 Quick / 580 Standard / 120 Master, split 400 Thesis / 300 Seminars / 300 Study.
Recommended: keep as proposed.
