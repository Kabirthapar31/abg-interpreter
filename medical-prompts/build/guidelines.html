01 · Research

What the four AI labs agree on

I read the official prompting guides from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Microsoft. Their vocabulary differs, but they agree on the core, and any prompt that follows these principles works well in any chatbot.

#PrincipleOpenAIAnthropicGoogleMicrosoft
1Be clear, specific and direct. Vague prompts get generic answers.✔✔✔✔
2Give the model a role or persona.✔✔✔✔
3Give context: who you are, the purpose, the audience and the constraints.✔✔✔✔
4State the output format: structure, length and tone.✔✔✔✔
5Separate the sections with delimiters (### or XML tags).✔✔✔✔
6Use examples (few-shot) when format or style matters.✔✔✔✔
7Break complex tasks into ordered steps or a chain of prompts.✔✔✔✔
8Say what to do, not only what to avoid.✔✔✔✔
9Ground the answer in the source material and name the source.✔✔✔✔
10Iterate: test, refine, and use follow-up turns.✔✔✔✔
11Avoid contradictory instructions. New models follow instructions literally.✔✔––
02 · Frameworks

Each lab's framework in one line

GOOGLE GEMINI

PTCF: Persona · Task · Context · Format

Google reports that successful Workspace prompts average about 21 words, and that complex requests need longer, more detailed prompts. Its advice is to write in natural language, as if talking to a person.

MICROSOFT COPILOT

GCES: Goal · Context · Expectations · Source

A goal alone is enough to get an answer. Context, expectations (format, tone, length) and a named source make the answer more specific and better grounded.

OPENAI CHATGPT

Identity → Instructions → Examples → Context

Put instructions first and separate the sections with ### or XML. GPT-5 follows instructions "with surgical precision", so contradictions hurt more than they used to. Length and verbosity can be set in plain language.

ANTHROPIC CLAUDE

Clear & direct · explain why · XML · examples

Treat the AI as "a brilliant but new employee who lacks context". Explain the why behind each instruction, give 3–5 diverse examples, put long documents first and the question last (up to 30% better), and ask it to quote the source before answering.

MEDICAL LITERATURE

JMIR 2025 clinician tutorial · medical-education papers

These describe the same four pillars (Role, Context, Task, Output), add "think step by step" for clinical reasoning, and warn about phantom citations: every AI-generated reference has to be checked in PubMed or another verified database.

04 · Length & context

How many words, and how much context?

None of the labs sets a single ideal word count. Google's ~21-word average describes quick everyday requests, and all four labs say complex tasks need more detail. The library therefore uses three tiers:

Quick

30–60 words

One focused output: a mnemonic, 10 MCQs, one differential. Role + Task + Format in 2–4 sentences.

Standard · default

80–150 words

Most prompts: a thesis section, a seminar outline, a study plan. All six blocks as short labelled lines.

Deep

150–300 words

Multi-step work: a full ROL framework, a protocol, a mock viva. Numbered steps and an optional example.

Five rules for context

  1. Enough to remove guesswork, and no more. Give 2–5 facts the AI can't infer: your level (MBBS / PG year / consultant), specialty, topic, deadline or exam, audience, and the standard you need followed (ICMJE, CONSORT, STROBE, PRISMA, CARE, Vancouver).
  2. Explain the why. "…because this is for my MD thesis committee" gives a better result than "be formal". (Anthropic)
  3. Fill-in blanks go in [SQUARE BRACKETS], e.g. [SPECIALTY], [TOPIC], [STUDY DESIGN], [EXAM]. Keep it to 5 or fewer per prompt.
  4. Pasted material goes first and the instruction goes last. When the user pastes a paper or data, the paste slot sits above the final instruction. (Anthropic long-context rule)
  5. No contradictions. Never "be concise" and "be exhaustive" in the same prompt. (OpenAI GPT-5 guide)
05 · Format rules

How each prompt is written

Start with an action verb

Draft, Build, Critique, Convert, Simulate, Compare, Tabulate. It tells the AI exactly what kind of work to do.

Phrase instructions positively

"Write in formal academic prose" rather than "Don't be casual". All four labs recommend this.

Number the steps

Whenever order or completeness matters, list the steps 1), 2), 3) so none get skipped.

Name the output shape

"A table with columns X | Y | Z", "8–10 slides with speaker notes", "300 words", "5 viva questions with model answers".

Plain labelled lines

Role: / Context: / Task: … These paste cleanly into any chat app and every model parses them reliably. XML is optional for advanced users.

One prompt, one job

A big job becomes a chain (Prompt 1 → 2 → 3) rather than one giant prompt, as both Anthropic and OpenAI recommend.

Interaction hook. Where it helps, prompts end with "Ask me 3 clarifying questions before you start" or "End by suggesting the next step", so the chat keeps improving instead of stopping at a first draft.
06 · Medical safety & accuracy

Mandatory guards for medical prompts

Every research or clinical prompt includes at least one of these:

GuardWording used in prompts
Citation guard"Do not invent references. Only cite papers you are confident exist, give the DOI/PMID, and mark anything uncertain as [VERIFY]." Users are told to check every reference in PubMed.
Uncertainty permission"If you are unsure or the evidence conflicts, say so explicitly."
Guideline anchoring"As per the latest [ACC/AHA / NICE / WHO / ICMR] guideline…"
Reasoning request"Reason step by step, showing how each finding changes the probability of each diagnosis."
Privacy"Use de-identified details only." No patient names, IDs or dates.
Academic integrityThesis and manuscript prompts help users structure, critique and improve their own work. They never ask the AI to fabricate data or results, and they remind users to follow their institution's and journal's AI-disclosure policy.
Clinical disclaimerDiagnostic prompts are for learning and decision support, not a substitute for clinical judgement.

08 · Worked examples

One example from each part of the library

A · Thesis & Research: methodology chapter (Standard)

Role: You are a senior medical research methodologist and thesis guide.
Context: I am a PG resident in [SPECIALTY]. My thesis topic is [TOPIC], a [STUDY DESIGN] with
[SAMPLE SIZE] patients. My committee wants a clear, well-justified methodology chapter.
Task: Draft the methodology outline: 1) study design and setting, 2) inclusion/exclusion
criteria, 3) sample size justification, 4) variables and definitions, 5) data collection,
6) statistical plan, 7) ethics.
Specifics: Align with the [STROBE/CONSORT] checklist. Flag any gap in my design. Do not
invent references; mark uncertain ones [VERIFY].
Format: Numbered headings with 2–4 bullet points each, formal academic tone, ~400 words.
Next: Ask me for any missing details before finalising.

B · Seminars & Journal Clubs: journal club presentation (Standard)

Role: You are an experienced journal-club moderator and critical-appraisal expert.
Context: I am presenting the paper below at our [SPECIALTY] department journal club to
residents and faculty.
[PASTE ABSTRACT / FULL TEXT HERE]
Task: 1) Summarise the PICO, 2) appraise it using the [CASP/JBI] checklist, 3) list 3
strengths and 3 limitations, 4) state the clinical bottom line, 5) suggest 5 discussion
questions.
Format: 12–15 slide outline with slide titles, bullet content and one-line speaker notes.
Next: End with the 3 questions faculty are most likely to ask me, with short answers.

C · Study & Exam Prep: mock viva (Deep)

Role: You are a strict but fair [SPECIALTY] examiner for the [EXAM] viva.
Context: I am a final-year PG preparing for my viva in [WEEKS] weeks. My weak area is [TOPIC].
Task: Conduct a mock viva. Ask one question at a time, starting basic and escalating to
examiner-level. After each of my answers: 1) score it /10, 2) give the ideal answer in 3–5
points, 3) name the gap in my reasoning, then ask the next question.
Specifics: Base standards on current guidelines ([GUIDELINE]). If a fact is
guideline-dependent or debated, say so.
Format: Conversational. Keep each question under 40 words.
Next: After 10 questions, summarise my weak areas and give a 3-day revision plan.
09 · Quality control

Checklist every prompt must pass

  • Has a Role, Context, Task and Format (the four elements every lab agrees on)
  • Task starts with an action verb and is clear enough that a colleague could follow it (Anthropic's "golden rule")
  • Falls in the right word tier: Quick 30–60 / Standard 80–150 / Deep 150–300
  • Placeholders are in [BRACKETS], 5 or fewer
  • No contradictory instructions
  • Output shape is named (table, slides, word count, number of items)
  • Includes a medical accuracy guard where relevant (citations, guidelines, uncertainty)
  • Works unchanged in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Copilot
  • Doesn't duplicate another prompt in the library
  • Positive phrasing: says what to do
