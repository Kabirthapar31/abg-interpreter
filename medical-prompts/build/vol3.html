Before you start

Read this first.

These prompts are written the way a research supervisor thinks, not the way a chatbot answers. Many are long on purpose: a vague prompt gets you a vague, confident, wrong answer. Before you paste anything, five non-negotiables.

01
The AI does not know your patients.Every prompt has a context block. Fill it honestly. If you say you have 200 cases a year when you have 40, the model will design a study you cannot finish.
02
Never accept a citation you have not opened.Language models fabricate references that look flawless: real journal, real authors, wrong or non-existent paper. Verify every reference on PubMed by PMID or DOI before it enters your work.
03
Never paste identifiable patient data.No names, hospital IDs, MRNs or scans with burned-in identifiers. De-identify before the data touches a chatbot. This is an ethics committee issue, not a preference.
04
The AI drafts. You decide.Use it to speed up structure, phrasing and pre-checking. The scientific judgement, the assumptions and the interpretation stay yours. Your name goes on the paper, not the model's.
05
Declare AI use where required.ICMJE and most journals require disclosure of generative AI use. AI cannot be an author. Check your journal's policy and your university's thesis rules before submission.

How to use each prompt

  • Copy the full prompt. The length is what makes the output specific.
  • Replace every [SQUARE BRACKET] with your real details. Options inside a bracket are separated by "/"; keep the one that applies.
  • Put anything you paste (abstracts, drafts, results) where the prompt shows [PASTE …], above the task.
  • Use a model with web search or document upload switched on wherever the prompt asks for evidence.
  • Run each stage in a fresh chat so the model doesn't carry assumptions from earlier steps.
  • Push back. If the output is generic, reply: "Be more specific. Tie every point to my context."
The framework

Every prompt is built on P-R-O-M-P-T.

One formula, six layers. Each layer matches what OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Microsoft recommend in their own prompting guides.

P
Persona

R
Request
One clear task, as numbered steps
O
Output
Shape, length, tone, tables
M
Material
Your context and anything you paste
P

T
Tweak
Self-critique and the next step

701Structured topic master for any subject
Master
3.1 Learning any topic · MBBS Student / PG Resident
Use itFor deep revision of any topic in any subject, before university exams, NEET-PG or UPSC CMS.
WhyScattered notes from five sources waste revision time. One structured, exam-ready note per topic (definition to complications, with high-yield points and examiner favourites flagged) becomes your master copy for every revision cycle.
COPY THE PROMPT BELOW
Persona
You are an expert medical educator in [SUBJECT] who prepares students for university examinations, NEET-PG and UPSC CMS. You write structured, exam-ready notes, use tables wherever comparison helps, and flag what examiners love to ask.
My context
Audience and time available: [brief]
What I most want to get out of this: [one line]
Topic: [e.g. primary open-angle glaucoma / nephrotic syndrome / brachial plexus]
My level: [MBBS year / intern / PG resident in SPECIALTY]
Exam I'm preparing for: [university theory / NEET-PG / UPSC CMS / MD-MS-DNB final / USMLE / PLAB]
Standard textbook I use: [name and edition]
Time I can spend on this topic: [e.g. 2 hours]
Task
1. Definition and classification.
2. Aetiology and risk factors.
3. Pathophysiology, with the key mechanisms as a flow.
4. Clinical features: symptoms, signs, and grading or staging scales where applicable.
5. Investigations, with interpretation pearls.
6. Management: medical, surgical and procedural, in a stepwise format.
7. Complications and their management.
8. High-yield exam points, previous-year question themes, and commonly asked viva questions with model answers.
9. A one-line "if you remember only one thing" summary.
Rules
Keep facts consistent with standard textbooks and current guidelines, and mark any drug dose, cut-off or guideline-dependent statement as [VERIFY FROM TEXTBOOK / GUIDELINE], because exam answers must match the standard source. Flag anything that is a favourite examiner question. Say so where textbooks disagree.
Output
Headed sections with bullets and tables, then the high-yield box and the viva questions with answers.
Tweak
End with 5 active-recall questions I should answer tomorrow without looking at the notes.

How to get more out of it

  • Replace [TOPIC] with anything: retinal detachment, uveitis, heart failure, brachial plexus.
  • Cross-check the output against your standard textbook once, then revise only from this note.
  • Answer the 5 recall questions the next day. That's what turns reading into remembering.

702A personal tutor that checks my understanding
Master
3.1 Learning any topic · MBBS Student / PG Resident
Use itWhen a topic feels confusing and you want to understand it, not just read it.
WhyReading explanations feels like learning but often isn't. A tutor that explains in small parts, asks you a question after each part, and moves on only when you answer correctly forces real understanding, and shows you exactly where your gaps are.
COPY THE PROMPT BELOW
Persona
You are an expert, patient tutor in [SUBJECT] who teaches using the Socratic method. You explain simply, use real-world and clinical analogies, and never move ahead until the learner shows understanding.
My context
Topic: [TOPIC]
My level and what I already know: [brief]
Exam or purpose: [university exam / NEET-PG / UPSC CMS / residency exam / clinical work]
Where I usually get confused: [brief or "not sure"]
Task
1. Break the topic into 5–7 small, logical parts, and show me the list first.
2. Teach one part at a time in simple language, with an analogy and a clinical example.
3. After each part, ask me one question that tests understanding, not recall.
4. Wait for my answer. If it's correct, confirm it and add one deeper point; if it's wrong, explain the misconception differently and ask another question.
5. Move to the next part only when I answer correctly.
6. At the end, summarise the topic in 5 bullet points.
7. Give me 3 exam-style questions to test myself, with answers hidden until I attempt them.
Rules
Ask only one question at a time and wait for my reply, because the learning happens in my answering. Keep facts accurate to standard textbooks and mark guideline-dependent details [VERIFY]. Don't give me the answers to your check questions before I try.
Output
Conversational: one part and one question per turn, then the final summary and exam questions.
Tweak
At the end, tell me which part I struggled with most and suggest one resource type to strengthen it.

How to get more out of it

  • Answer honestly, including "I don't know". The tutor adapts to your answers.
  • Ask "explain it differently" whenever an explanation doesn't click.
  • Revisit the part you struggled with after 2 days. Spacing makes it stick.

703Build a custom tutor that answers only from my notes
Master
3.1 Learning any topic · MBBS Student / PG Resident
Use itWhen setting up a Custom GPT (ChatGPT), a Claude Project or a Gemini Gem with your own notes and textbooks.
WhyA general chatbot mixes your standard textbook with random internet content. A custom tutor instructed to answer only from your uploaded material, cite the file and page, and say "not in your notes" when it doesn't know gives exam-consistent answers you can trust and check.
COPY THE PROMPT BELOW
Persona
You are an expert at configuring AI study assistants for medical exams. You write system instructions that keep the assistant grounded in the uploaded files and in exam style.
My context
Tool: [ChatGPT Custom GPT / Claude Project / Gemini Gem]
Exam: [NEET-PG / UPSC CMS / MD-MS-DNB final / university exam]
Files I will upload (textbooks, notes, PYQs): [list]
How I want to use it: [Q&A / viva drill / MCQ practice / revision summaries]
Task
1. Write the complete system instructions for the tutor: answer ONLY from the uploaded files; if the answer isn't in the files, reply "Not in your uploaded notes. Verify from a textbook."; always cite the file name and page or section; use exam-style language; highlight high-yield facts and previous-year traps.
2. Add modes the tutor can switch between on command: "Explain", "Viva", "MCQ", "Summary" and "Compare", each with its rules.
3. Add instructions for handling conflicting information between files.
4. Write 5 starter prompts I can use daily.
5. Explain how to test the tutor on 5 questions whose answers I already know, to check its grounding.
Rules
Keep the instructions within typical length limits for the tool and mark tool-specific settings as [CHECK CURRENT SETTINGS], because platform features change often. Upload only material I'm allowed to use.
Output
The system instructions in a code block, the mode definitions, the conflict rule, the starter prompts, then the testing procedure.
Tweak
End with the 3 warning signs that the tutor is answering from outside my files, and what to do.

How to get more out of it

  • Test the tutor with a question you know isn't in your notes. It should say so.
  • Keep one tutor per subject. Mixed subjects confuse retrieval.
  • Upload your previous-year papers too, so it can link topics to past questions.

705Understand a mechanism from first principles
Master
3.1 Learning any topic · MBBS Student / PG Resident
Use itWhen you have memorised something but can't explain why it happens.
WhyMechanisms learned from first principles don't need to be memorised: you can reconstruct them in the exam hall. "Why" chains, from a clinical feature back to physiology, turn isolated facts into understanding that survives pressure and tricky questions.
COPY THE PROMPT BELOW
Persona
You are a physiology-minded clinician-teacher who explains every clinical fact by asking "why" until reaching basic principles.
My context
My specialty and year: [e.g. final-year MBBS / MD Medicine, year 2]
Audience and time available: [brief]
What I most want to get out of this: [one line]
The fact or phenomenon I want to understand: [e.g. why hypokalaemia causes muscle weakness, why the JVP rises in right heart failure]
My level: [brief]
The basic science I'm comfortable with: [brief]
Task
1. Start from the clinical fact and build a "why" chain of 4–6 steps down to the basic principle.
2. Explain each step in plain language with a simple analogy.
3. Draw the chain as a flow diagram in text, with arrows between the steps.
4. Show how the same principle explains 2–3 other clinical facts.
5. Give 3 "predict the effect" questions that test whether I can apply the principle.
6. Link the principle to one treatment and explain why the treatment works.
7. Point out one common exam question that this principle answers, and show the answer built from the chain.
7. Finish with a short list of anything you were unsure about, so I can verify it before use.
Rules
Keep each step physiologically accurate and mark any simplification as "simplified", because oversimplified mechanisms cause errors in harder questions. Mark anything textbook-dependent [VERIFY].
Output
The why-chain, the explanations, the flow diagram, the connected facts, the prediction questions, then the treatment link.
Tweak
End with a one-sentence version of the principle I can say in a viva.

How to get more out of it

  • Try to answer each "why" yourself before reading the explanation.
  • One principle often explains ten facts. Find the principle and you save hours.
  • Draw the chain from memory the next day.

706One topic across all subjects: vertical integration
Master
3.1 Learning any topic · MBBS Student / PG Resident
Use itWhen you want to see how a topic connects across anatomy, physiology, pathology, pharmacology, microbiology and clinical medicine.
WhyExams, especially NEET-PG and clinical vivas, increasingly test integration: a question about a disease may ask about its anatomy, mechanism or drug. Studying one topic vertically across subjects builds connected knowledge that is easier to recall.
COPY THE PROMPT BELOW
Persona
You are a medical educator who designs integrated, organ-system teaching across preclinical, paraclinical and clinical subjects.
My context
My specialty and year: [e.g. final-year MBBS / MD Medicine, year 2]
Audience and time available: [brief]
What I most want to get out of this: [one line]
Topic or disease: [e.g. myocardial infarction / tuberculosis / diabetes mellitus]
My year and exam: [brief]
Subjects to cover: [all / list]
Task
1. Build an integration map: for each subject (anatomy, physiology, biochemistry, pathology, microbiology, pharmacology, community medicine, forensic medicine if relevant, and clinical medicine or surgery), list the 3–5 high-yield points about this topic.
2. Show the links between subjects (e.g. coronary anatomy → infarct territory → ECG leads → complications).
3. Write 5 integrated questions that cross at least 2 subjects each, with answers.
4. Suggest a one-page visual map of the connections, with the disease in the centre.
5. Mark the facts most often asked in NEET-PG and UPSC CMS style questions, as recurring themes, not claimed statistics.
6. Suggest the order in which to revise the subjects for this topic, and how long each should take.
6. Finish with a short list of anything you were unsure about, so I can verify it before use.
Rules
Keep facts consistent with standard textbooks and mark doses and cut-offs [VERIFY], because integrated questions punish small factual errors.
Output
The integration table by subject, the link list, the integrated questions with answers, the map description, then the high-yield marks.
Tweak
End with the 3 cross-subject links students most often miss.

How to get more out of it

  • Use this for the 20 most important diseases. It covers a surprising share of the syllabus.
  • Draw the map by hand. It sticks better than a printed one.
  • Revise the integrated questions before mock tests.

714A learning roadmap from my level to my goal
Standard
3.1 Learning any topic · MBBS Student / PG Resident

Persona
You are a curriculum designer who builds realistic learning roadmaps.
My context
Subject or skill: [e.g. ECG interpretation]
Current level: [brief]
Goal: [e.g. confidently interpret common ECGs for the NEET-PG image-based questions]
Time available: [weeks and hours per week]
Task
1. Break the path into 4–6 stages with prerequisites.
2. For each stage: learning goals, resources by type, practice tasks and a measurable checkpoint.
3. Estimate the time per stage.

Studying with AI, safely
  • Verify every fact, number and drug dose against a standard textbook or current guideline.
  • Use AI to test yourself, not to avoid thinking. Answer first, then check.
  • Never use AI during an exam or assessment where it isn't allowed.
  • Clinical reasoning prompts are for learning, not for decisions about real patients.
