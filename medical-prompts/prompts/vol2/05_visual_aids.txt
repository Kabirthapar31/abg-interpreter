# 2.5 | Posters & visual teaching aids | Ward protocol posters, infographics, patient leaflets, pocket cards and AI-generated illustrations: visuals teach faster than words, when they are accurate and simple.

@@ M | ward-protocol-poster | Clinical protocol poster for a ward or ICU
aud: PG Resident / Faculty / Nursing educator
use: When your unit adopts a protocol (sepsis bundle, hypoglycaemia, anaphylaxis, blood transfusion) and staff need it at a glance.
why: Protocols in files are not followed; protocols on the wall, clear enough to use at 3 a.m., are. A well-designed poster turns a guideline into action, provided every dose and threshold is verified and it matches your hospital's approved protocol.
persona: You are a clinical quality-improvement lead and medical illustrator who designs bedside protocol posters that nurses and doctors actually use in emergencies.
context: Protocol topic: [e.g. management of hypoglycaemia in adult inpatients]
Source: our hospital's approved protocol or guideline: [UPLOAD / PASTE]
Users: [nurses / residents / all staff]
Where it will hang, and the size: [e.g. nursing station, A3]
Language(s): [English / Hindi / bilingual]
Drugs and equipment available locally: [list]
task: 1. Extract the steps that must be on the poster: recognition criteria, immediate actions, drug doses and routes, monitoring, escalation triggers and who to call.
2. Design a top-to-bottom flow: a trigger box, action steps, decision points (yes/no), and escalation.
3. Write the text for each box in under 12 words, using action verbs.
4. Specify the design: large fonts, a colour code (red for emergency actions), icons and plenty of white space.
5. Add a footer with the source protocol, version and date, the approving authority, and the review date.
6. Write a 5-minute staff briefing to launch the poster.
rules: Take every dose, threshold and step only from the approved protocol I provided, and mark each [VERIFY AGAINST APPROVED PROTOCOL] until it is checked, because a wrong dose on a wall poster can harm patients. Match the local drug formulation and route. Get sign-off from the unit in-charge before printing.
output: The content extraction, the flow description (box by box), the design specification, the footer, then the briefing script.
tweak: End with a checklist for testing the poster: can a new nurse follow it correctly in 60 seconds?
tips:
- Test the poster with 3 staff members before printing. Watch where they hesitate.
- Laminate it and put a review date on it. Outdated protocol posters are dangerous.
- Put the poster where the action happens, not where there's space.

persona: You are a medical educator and visual designer who writes precise image-generation prompts for educational comics, and who checks every clinical fact before it goes into an image.
context: Topic: [e.g. hypokalaemia: 5 things you must know]

Character A (the asker): [e.g. a curious junior doctor in a white coat with a stethoscope, holding a clipboard]
Character B (the answerer): [e.g. a calm, confident senior physician in scrubs]
Brand colours and handle: [e.g. black and orange, @yourhandle]
Image tool: [ChatGPT image / Gemini / other]
task: 1. Check each of my 5 Q&A pairs for accuracy and brevity (answers under 12 words), and flag anything to verify.
2. Assign a tone to character A (curious, puzzled, worried, sceptical, alarmed, thinking) and an emotion to character B (reassuring, serious, knowing smile, warning finger) for each panel.
3. Write the complete image prompt: a single vertical comic-style infographic (portrait, 4:5), the title bar "[TOPIC]: 5 Things You Must Know", a clean modern medical-comic style with bold outlines, flat cell-shaded colouring and a soft palette, the brand colours on the title bar and panel borders, and 5 stacked numbered panels, each with both characters, a question bubble and an answer bubble.
4. Specify that both characters look identical in every panel, the text is short and legible, nothing is cropped, and a footer bar shows my handle.
5. Write 2 fix-up prompts for common problems: garbled text in one panel, and characters that drift between panels.
rules: Put only facts I have verified into the image, and flag doubtful ones as [VERIFY] before generating, because errors in viral images can't be corrected once shared. Keep each bubble's text short, because image tools garble long text.
output: The accuracy check, the tone table, the full image prompt in a code block, then the fix-up prompts.
tweak: End with a caption of under 60 words for posting the comic, with 2 references to cite.
tips:
- Fix one panel at a time: "Fix the spelling in panel 3 only, keep everything else."
- Keep answers under 12 words. Longer text is where image tools make spelling errors.
- Read the generated image letter by letter before posting. Image tools still misspell medical terms.
