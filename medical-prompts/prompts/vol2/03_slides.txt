# 2.3 | Slide design & structure | Slides support the speaker; they are not the speech. One message per slide, visuals over paragraphs, and text readable from the back row.

@@ M | deck-redesign | Redesign my existing deck, slide by slide
aud: PG Resident / Faculty
use: When your deck is text-heavy, cluttered or inconsistent, and the talk is in a few days.
why: Most medical decks are written like documents: full sentences, tiny fonts, six colours and tables pasted from papers. A slide-by-slide redesign into message titles, one idea per slide and simple visuals transforms the talk, without changing a single fact.
persona: You are a presentation designer who specialises in medical and scientific talks. You apply the assertion-evidence approach (a message title plus visual evidence), and you are strict about readability and consistency.
context: My deck (paste the slide text, or upload the .pptx or PDF): [PASTE OR UPLOAD]
Talk type, audience and duration: [e.g. departmental seminar, residents, 40 minutes]
Venue and screen: [e.g. seminar room projector / hall / online]
Template or branding rules: [institution template or "free"]
Tool I will edit in: [PowerPoint / Google Slides / Keynote / Canva]
task: 1. Diagnose the deck as a whole: text density, font sizes, colour consistency, visual hierarchy and slide count against the time.
2. For every slide, give: the problem, a message title (a full-sentence takeaway), the redesigned content (maximum 3 bullets, or a visual), and what moves to the speaker notes.
3. Identify slides to merge, split or delete.
4. Suggest a visual for each text-heavy slide: flowchart, table-to-chart, icon grid, timeline or annotated image.
5. Propose a consistent style: 2 fonts at most, a 3-colour palette plus neutrals, fixed title positions and a grid.
6. Give the edit steps in my tool (master slides, layout reuse, alignment tools).
rules: Keep every fact, number and reference exactly as in my deck, because the redesign must change the presentation, not the content. Mark anything that looks factually doubtful as [CHECK] rather than correcting it. Keep body text at 24 pt or more and titles at 32 pt or more for a projector.
output: The overall diagnosis, a slide-by-slide table (slide, problem, new title, new content, notes), the merge and delete list, the style guide, then the edit steps.
tweak: End with the 3 slides whose redesign will make the biggest difference, and before-and-after text for each.
tips:
- Redesign the master slide first. Every slide improves at once.
- Read only the slide titles in order. If they tell the story, the redesign has worked.
- Keep the old deck. If time runs out, present the 10 most important redesigned slides.

aud: PG Resident / Faculty
use: When your talk includes results, statistics or comparisons that currently sit in dense tables.
why: A 12-column table on a slide is unreadable from the third row of the audience. Choosing the right chart, stripping the clutter and highlighting the one number that matters turns data into a message people remember.

The message each table should convey: [list, or "suggest"]
Tool: [PowerPoint / Excel / Google Sheets / Canva]
Audience and room size: [brief]
task: 1. For each table, identify the single message it supports.
2. Recommend the best visual for that message (bar, dot plot, line, forest-style plot, simple highlighted table, or a single big number), with the reason.
3. Specify the design: sorted order, direct labels instead of legends, minimal grid lines, one highlight colour and a message title.
4. Say what to remove: extra decimals, unnecessary columns, legends that could be direct labels, and 3D effects.
5. Give step-by-step build instructions in my tool.
6. Write the message title and a one-line speaker note for each visual.
7. Flag any table that should stay a table because readers need the exact values.
7. Finish with a short list of anything you were unsure about, so I can verify it before use.
rules: Keep every number exactly as in my data, and keep the axes honest (bars start at zero), because distorted charts mislead the audience and damage your credibility.
output: A table (original table, message, recommended visual, design notes), the build steps, then the titles and notes.
tweak: End with the one chart that best tells the main story of the talk.
tips:
- If only one number matters, show only that number, in very large font, with context.
- Sort bars by value, not alphabetically, unless the order has meaning.
- Use colour to highlight, not to decorate. Grey everything except the key bar.
