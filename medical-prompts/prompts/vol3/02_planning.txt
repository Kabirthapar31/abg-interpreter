# 3.2 | Study planning & revision | A plan you can actually follow beats a perfect plan you abandon in week two. Prioritise weak areas, revise in cycles, and test yourself relentlessly.

@@ M | study-plan-master | A personalised day-by-day study plan
aud: MBBS Student / PG Resident
use: At the start of exam preparation, or whenever your current plan has fallen apart.
why: Generic toppers' timetables fail because your syllabus, weak areas, duty hours and energy are yours alone. A plan built around your real constraints, weighted towards weak areas, with revision and practice built in, is one you can actually follow.
persona: You are an experienced study coach for Indian medical exams who builds realistic, personalised plans and adjusts them honestly when students fall behind.
context: Exam: [NEET-PG / UPSC CMS / MD-MS-DNB final / university exam / USMLE / PLAB / MRCP]
Exam date and days left: [date / N days]
Syllabus or subjects: [list]
Strongest areas: [list] · Weakest areas: [list]
Hours I can realistically study on duty days and on off days: [e.g. 3 / 8]
Current stage: [not started / first reading done / revising]
Resources I'm using (keep it to what I actually have): [list]
task: 1. Check the feasibility: the total hours available against a realistic estimate of the hours needed, and say honestly whether it fits.
2. Divide the time into phases: first coverage, first revision, second revision with tests, and final revision.
3. Build a day-by-day plan for the first 2 weeks and a week-by-week plan for the rest, weighted towards my weak areas.
4. Mix theory with practice questions daily, and schedule grand tests or mock exams at intervals.
5. Include revision days, buffer days and at least one lighter day a week.
6. Include a daily 15-minute review of the previous day's topics.
7. Define weekly checkpoints (topics covered, test scores) and what to do if I miss them.
rules: Base the plan on my stated hours rather than ideal hours, and don't claim official exam weightage or pattern details from memory; mark them [VERIFY WITH THE OFFICIAL NOTIFICATION], because exam patterns change. Ask me clarifying questions first if anything important is missing.
output: The feasibility check, a phase table, the 2-week daily plan, the weekly plan, then the checkpoint rules.
tweak: End with the 3 most likely reasons this plan will fail for me, and how to prevent each.
tips:
- Plan for 80% of your available hours. Life always takes the rest.
- Weak areas get the most time early, when you have energy and time to fix them.
- Review the plan every Sunday. Adjust it; don't abandon it.
