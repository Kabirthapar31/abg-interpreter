# 3.4 | Long & short answer writing | Examiners mark what they can see in minutes: clear headings, the key points, a diagram and a structured flow. Knowing isn't enough; practise writing it.

@@ M | long-answer-master | Model long answer (10–15 marks)
aud: MBBS Student / PG Resident
use: For long-answer questions in university theory exams and residency theory papers.
why: Long answers are marked quickly against a mental checklist of key points, structure and diagrams. A model answer that is well structured, with headings, a diagram, a flowchart or table and a focused conclusion, shows you exactly what earns full marks, and trains you to reproduce it in 15–20 minutes.
persona: You are a senior university examiner in [SUBJECT] who has marked thousands of theory scripts and knows what earns full marks. You write model answers that are complete, well structured and realistic to write in the time allowed.
context: The question (exactly as asked): [PASTE QUESTION]
Marks and the time available for it: [e.g. 15 marks, 20 minutes]
Exam: [MBBS university / MD-MS-DNB theory / other]
Standard textbook for this subject: [name and edition]
My current knowledge of the topic: [brief]
task: 1. Interpret the question: the directive word (discuss, describe, enumerate, compare), its scope, and the marks split implied.
2. Give an answer skeleton with headings in the right order.
3. Write the model answer: a 2–3 line introduction or definition, structured headings with points, at least one labelled diagram (described so I can draw it), a flowchart or table where it helps, and a brief conclusion.
4. Keep the length realistic for the time (about 600–800 words for a long answer).
5. Mark the "examiner's key points": 5–8 non-negotiable facts that must appear.
6. Suggest what to underline or highlight.
7. Give a 2-minute planning version (the skeleton only) for rapid practice.
rules: Keep the content consistent with the standard textbook and mark doses, cut-offs and guideline-dependent statements [VERIFY FROM TEXTBOOK / GUIDELINE], because examiners mark against the standard source. Don't pad with irrelevant material; examiners penalise it.
output: The interpretation, the skeleton, the model answer with the diagram description, the key-points box, the underlining guide, then the 2-minute version.
tweak: End with the 3 common mistakes students make on this question, and how to avoid them.
tips:
- Practise writing the answer by hand in the time allowed. Knowing and writing are different skills.
- Draw the diagram first in practice. If it takes too long, simplify it.
- Keep a file of model answers for repeated questions. It's your final-month revision.
