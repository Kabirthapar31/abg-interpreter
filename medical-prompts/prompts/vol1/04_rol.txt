# 1.4 | Review of Literature | A literature review is not a summary of ten papers you happened to find. It is a reproducible search, an evidence table and a defensible gap statement.

@@ M | rol-screen-extract | Screen, extract and synthesise: the ROL engine
aud: PG Resident / Faculty
use: After your search is complete (section 1.2), when you have a pile of records to turn into a review.
why: Most thesis ROLs are a string of study summaries. Examiners want to see explicit criteria, a structured evidence table, a quality assessment and a synthesis that ends on the gap. This prompt builds all four from your own papers.
persona: You are a systematic-review methodologist who has co-authored Cochrane reviews and supervised dozens of postgraduate literature reviews. You are sceptical of weak evidence and insist on explicit, reproducible decisions.
context: My PICOT question: [PASTE QUESTION]
Study designs I expect to find: [e.g. cohorts, case-control, a few RCTs]
Number of records after deduplication: [N]
Papers I have uploaded to this chat: [list, or "none yet"]
task: Part A: screen and extract
1. Draft explicit inclusion and exclusion criteria, phrased so a second reviewer could apply them without asking me questions.
2. Design a data-extraction table: author, year, country, setting, design, sample size, population, exposure or intervention, comparator, outcome definition, effect estimate with 95% CI, follow-up, funding, key limitation.
3. Name the right risk-of-bias tool for each design I will encounter, and list its domains.
Part B: synthesise
4. Give a skeleton for the review narrative: how to open, how to group studies by theme rather than chronology, how to handle conflicting findings, how to close on the gap.
5. Show how to write a three-sentence gap statement: what is known, what is missing, why it matters in my setting.
6. Draft a PRISMA-style flow description with placeholder numbers.
rules: Do not give any specific citation, author, journal, year, sample size or result unless I uploaded that paper or you retrieved it through live search with a PMID or DOI, because an invented study in a thesis is academic misconduct. Where you describe what the literature broadly shows, label it [UNVERIFIED BACKGROUND — CONFIRM INDEPENDENTLY]. Never estimate a statistic from memory.
output: Criteria as two numbered lists, the extraction table as a column list, a tool table, the narrative skeleton, the gap-statement template, and the flow description.
tweak: End with the 3 decisions in this plan an examiner is most likely to question, and how to justify each.
tips:
- Upload the actual PDFs before asking for any comparison across papers. That is the difference between synthesis and fabrication.
- Pilot the extraction table on 3 papers before extracting all of them. Columns always need adjusting.
- Keep a list of excluded full texts with reasons. It feeds your PRISMA flow diagram.

task: 1. Propose 4–6 themes that organise the studies, and assign each study to a theme.
2. For each theme, write 2–4 paragraphs that compare studies: agreement, disagreement and the likely reasons (population, setting, definitions, design, sample size).
3. Include a short subsection on Indian studies, or say explicitly that Indian data is lacking in a theme.
4. End each theme with a sentence saying what it means for my study.
5. Close the chapter with a gap paragraph that leads to my objective.
6. After each theme, list the reference numbers used, so I can check coverage against my table.
rules: Use only the studies and numbers in my table, reproduced exactly with their reference numbers, because every figure will be checked against the source. tweak: Finish with the 3 places where my evidence is thinnest, so I can search again before submission.
tips:
- Write one theme per session. Long outputs lose accuracy.
- Spot-check 5 numbers in the draft against your PDFs before you trust the rest.
- Put your own interpretation into the closing sentence of each theme. That is where your voice matters most.

Tool I am using: [NotebookLM / Claude / ChatGPT / Gemini]
How I will use the table: [thesis ROL / systematic review / journal club]
task: 1. For each uploaded paper, extract every variable I listed into a table, one row per paper.
2. For each cell, give the supporting quotation from the paper with its section (Methods, Results, Table 2 and so on).
3. Write "NOT REPORTED" where the paper does not state the information.
4. After the table, list any inconsistency within a paper (e.g. abstract and results numbers differ).
