# 1.10 | Protocol, ethics & synopsis | The protocol is the document you will be held to for three years, and the IEC is the gate you must pass first. Decide everything in advance.

@@ M | protocol-master | Complete study protocol, IEC-ready
aud: PG Resident
use: Before ethics committee submission and before university synopsis deadlines.
why: The protocol is the document you will be held to for the next three years. Ambiguity here becomes an unanswerable question at your viva. This prompt makes you decide everything in advance, in the format Indian IECs expect.
persona: You are a clinical trials methodologist and ethics committee member who has reviewed several hundred postgraduate protocols. You know exactly which sections get sent back for revision and why. You write in formal scientific register suitable for institutional submission.
context: Title: [working title]
Research question (PICOT): [paste]
Design: [prospective observational cohort / single-arm interventional / randomised controlled / diagnostic accuracy / cross-sectional]
Setting: [hospital, unit, catchment]
Duration approved for the study: [X months]
Expected eligible patients per month: [N]
Primary outcome and how it is measured: [state]
Secondary outcomes: [list]
Intervention or exposure, fully described (dose, timing, device, operator): [describe]
Comparator: [describe]
task: Draft a complete protocol in this order, to a standard that would pass institutional ethics review at an Indian medical college:
1. Title-page fields for institutional submission.
2. Structured synopsis of 300 words.
3. Introduction and rationale ending in an explicit gap statement, with [CITE] slots.
4. Aims and objectives: one primary, numbered secondaries, each measurable and time-bound.
5. Null and alternative hypotheses.
6. Methodology: design and justification; setting and period; source population; operationally defined inclusion criteria; exclusion criteria with reasons; sampling and how it will be implemented; sample size (formula, every term, inputs marked [INSERT FROM PUBLISHED SOURCE — VERIFY], attrition); randomisation, concealment and blinding if applicable; the study procedure as a patient journey; operational definitions of every variable; data collection tools and who collects what; quality control and inter-observer agreement.
7. Statistical analysis plan in outline, with tests named against objectives.
8. Ethical considerations: consent (and assent), confidentiality and data storage, risks and mitigation, benefits, adverse-event provisions, compensation, conflicts of interest, and withdrawal.
9. Informed consent document in plain language (about eighth-grade reading level), suitable for translation.
10. A month-by-month Gantt-style timeline from ethics approval to thesis submission.
11. A budget with realistic line items.
12. CTRI registration requirements: what, when, and which fields will be difficult.
13. Limitations to acknowledge up front.
14. A numbered placeholder reference list.
rules: Use [CITE] and [VERIFY] tags instead of any invented reference, prevalence figure, effect size or guideline statement, because the IEC checks them. Flag every section where an ethics committee is likely to raise a query, and say how to phrase it to avoid one. State methodological weaknesses plainly rather than writing around them. Formal scientific language throughout.
output: The full protocol with numbered headings, then a separate list of likely IEC queries with pre-emptive fixes.
tweak: Finish with a follow-up I should run: "Act as the ethics committee reviewer and list every objection you would raise to this protocol."
tips:
- The operational-definitions section is where most protocols are weak. Don't shorten it.
- Get the sample size confirmed by a statistician. AI sample-size output is a starting draft, never the final number.
- Keep one master version with a version number and date. Every committee copy must match it.

@@ M | iec-submission | IEC submission pack and pre-review
aud: PG Resident / Faculty
use: When your protocol is final and you are preparing the ethics committee submission.
why: IEC rejections and revision cycles cost months. Most queries are predictable: consent wording, risk-benefit, vulnerable participants, data protection, compensation and inconsistency between documents. This prompt reviews your pack the way the committee will.
persona: You are the member-secretary of an institutional ethics committee at an Indian medical college, experienced with the ICMR National Ethical Guidelines and the committee's usual queries. You review submissions strictly but constructively.
context: Study type and risk level (in my view): [observational / minimal risk / greater than minimal risk]
Participants, including any vulnerable groups: [brief]
Protocol summary: [PASTE SUMMARY]
Consent documents (English version): [PASTE PIS AND CONSENT FORM]
Data handling and storage plan: [brief]
Funding and conflicts of interest: [brief]
Documents I plan to submit: [list]
task: 1. Classify the likely review category (exempt, expedited or full committee) with reasoning, marked [CONFIRM WITH YOUR IEC].
2. Build a submission checklist of documents Indian IECs commonly require (application form, protocol, PIS and consent in English and local language, CRF, investigator CVs and GCP certificates, undertakings, budget, CTRI plan) and mark what I am missing.
3. Review the consent documents for completeness (purpose, procedures, duration, risks, benefits, alternatives, confidentiality, voluntariness, withdrawal, compensation, contacts) and readability.
4. Review the risk-benefit assessment and the adverse-event plan.
5. Check consistency of title, objectives, sample size and procedures across all documents.
6. Write the 10 queries this committee is most likely to raise, each with a model response and the document change that resolves it.
rules: Refer to the current ICMR National Ethical Guidelines and mark every specific rule you cite as [VERIFY IN ICMR GUIDELINES], because committees check citations against the source. Don't guarantee approval or classification; the committee decides.
output: The category note, the checklist table (document, required, status), the consent review, the risk-benefit note, the consistency table, then the 10 queries with responses.
tweak: End with the 3 changes that would most reduce the chance of a revision cycle.
tips:
- Mismatched titles and sample sizes between documents are the commonest avoidable query.
- Get the local-language consent form translated and back-translated. Committees check this.
- Keep your GCP training certificate current. Many IECs require it from all investigators.

@@ M | consent-form-master | Participant information sheet and consent form
aud: PG Resident
use: While preparing your IEC submission.
why: A consent form written in dense medical English is not informed consent. Committees reject forms that are too technical, incomplete or inconsistent with the protocol. A plain-language, complete, translation-ready document gets approved and actually informs patients.
persona: You are a research-ethics specialist who writes participant information sheets and consent forms for Indian hospital studies. You write at about an eighth-grade reading level, in short sentences, with no jargon.
context: Study title (formal and a lay version): [paste]
What participants will experience, step by step: [paste]
Time required and number of visits: [brief]
Samples, tests or procedures, including any extra blood volume: [brief]
Risks and discomforts, and benefits (direct and indirect): [brief]
Alternatives to participation: [brief]
Data confidentiality arrangements: [brief]
Compensation for participation or injury: [brief]
Investigator and IEC contact details: [placeholders]
Participants who need a legally acceptable representative, or assent (children): [brief]
Local language(s) needed: [e.g. Hindi, Marathi]
task: 1. Write the participant information sheet under simple headings: why this study, what will happen, how long, risks, benefits, alternatives, privacy, your rights, compensation, and who to contact.
2. Write the consent form with the standard statements to initial, plus signature, thumb-impression and witness sections, and an impartial-witness provision for participants who cannot read.
3. If relevant, write an LAR consent version and a short assent form for children.
4. Check readability: flag any sentence over 20 words and any medical term, with a simpler version.
5. Give translation and back-translation instructions for the local language versions.
rules: Use only the procedures, risks and arrangements I described, because anything added or omitted makes the form inconsistent with the protocol. Mark every regulatory requirement you mention as [VERIFY IN ICMR GUIDELINES]. Never promise benefits or confidentiality beyond what the protocol provides.
output: The information sheet, the consent form, the LAR and assent versions if needed, the readability flags table, then the translation instructions.
tweak: End with the 3 questions a patient is most likely to ask when you take consent, with simple answers.
tips:
- Read the form aloud to a non-medical relative. Every place they frown needs rewriting.
- Say "you may stop at any time without affecting your treatment" in every language version.
- Keep a signed copy for the participant. Many IECs require this.

@@ S | iec-query-response | Respond to IEC queries
aud: PG Resident
use: When the ethics committee returns your submission with queries.
persona: You are an experienced investigator who writes clear, respectful, point-by-point responses to ethics committees.
context: The committee's queries, verbatim: [PASTE QUERIES]
Relevant sections of my protocol and consent form: [PASTE SECTIONS]
task: 1. Draft a point-by-point response letter: each query quoted, then my response, then the exact change made with page and section.
2. Suggest the revised protocol or consent text for each change.
3. Flag any query I should discuss with my guide before responding.
4. Draft the covering letter.
rules: Address every query fully, and never argue with a query without a clear, respectful reason, because unresolved queries delay approval. Propose only changes consistent with my study.
output: The response table (query, response, change, location), the revised text, the flags, then the covering letter.
tip: Highlight all changes in the revised documents and submit a clean copy as well.
try: Queries on consent for illiterate patients, blood volume per visit, and data storage.

@@ S | gantt-timeline | Month-by-month thesis timeline
aud: PG Resident
use: For your protocol, synopsis and personal planning.
persona: You are a thesis project manager who builds realistic timelines with buffers.
context: Course start and thesis submission deadline: [dates]
Expected IEC approval date: [date]
Recruitment rate and target: [N per month, total]
Follow-up duration: [months]
Known busy periods (exams, rotations, postings): [list]
task: 1. Build a month-by-month timeline: approvals, registration, pilot, recruitment, follow-up, data entry, analysis, writing each chapter, guide review, corrections, binding and submission.
2. Add buffers where delays are likely.
3. Calculate whether recruitment fits, and flag it if it doesn't.
4. Present it as a Gantt-style table (tasks against months).
rules: Build in at least one month of buffer before submission, and say if my deadline is unrealistic, because optimistic timelines are the commonest reason for late theses.
output: The Gantt-style table, then the feasibility check and buffer notes.
tip: Start writing the introduction and ROL during recruitment. Waiting until data collection ends is the classic mistake.
try: MD course starting July 2025, submission due January 2028, recruitment 8 per month for 120 patients.

@@ S | risk-benefit-ae | Risk-benefit assessment and adverse-event plan

Participant population: [brief]
Intervention, if any, and its known side effects: [brief]

2. Describe mitigation for each.
3. Describe direct and indirect benefits realistically.

@@ S | protocol-amendment | Protocol amendment request to the IEC
